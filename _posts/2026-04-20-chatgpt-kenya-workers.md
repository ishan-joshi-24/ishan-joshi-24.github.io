$2 an hour to read the worst things humans have ever written.

That's how OpenAI made ChatGPT safe.

In late 2021, OpenAI hired a San Francisco firm called Sama to help build a content filter. Sama's workers were in Nairobi, Kenya.

Their job was to label text. Violence. Abuse. Suicide. Self-harm. All of it pulled from the darkest corners of the internet.

They went through it for months. Tens of thousands of samples.

The only way to teach a model what "toxic" looks like is to show it thousands of hand-labeled examples. The filter that catches harmful outputs from ChatGPT was trained on human judgment. That judgment came from these workers.

OpenAI paid $12.50 an hour for the work.

The workers got $2.

Sama cancelled the contract eight months early. Workers reported nightmares, intrusive visions, broken relationships. They filed a petition with the Kenyan Parliament citing PTSD diagnoses.

The work didn't stop. Every frontier lab relies on it.

Every guardrail in ChatGPT has a human cost in the training data. We just don't see it.
