In 2006, NVIDIA made a bet that nobody understood.

They took a chip built to render pixels, the GPU, literally "Graphics Processing Unit", and said it could do more than graphics.

NVIDIA launched **CUDA**, a programming model that let developers use GPUs for general-purpose computing.

Wall Street didn't care. Developers didn't care. The stock was flat for years.

A handful of researchers used it for physics simulations and molecular modeling. That was it.

Then deep learning arrived.

Training neural networks needed massive parallel compute. Thousands of simple calculations running simultaneously.

Exactly what GPUs were built to do.

In 2012, AlexNet, a deep neural network, crushed the ImageNet competition, running on NVIDIA GPUs. That was the inflection point.

But here's the real genius.

NVIDIA's moat isn't the chip. It's CUDA.

A decade of libraries, tooling, frameworks, and developer adoption. PyTorch, TensorFlow, all optimized for CUDA first.

Competitors have shipped GPUs with better specs on paper. Doesn't matter. The ecosystem wins.

That's why NVIDIA went from a gaming company to a $3T giant.

The lesson? The best moats aren't built from hardware. They're built from developer adoption.

Sometimes the biggest bet is the one nobody else even sees as a bet.
