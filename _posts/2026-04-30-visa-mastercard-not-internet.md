When you tap your card, the transaction doesn't travel over the internet.

It never has.

Visa has **VisaNet**. Mastercard has **Banknet**. Both are private WANs, wide area networks built on leased lines connecting banks, processors, and merchants across the world.

They use the same language as the internet. Just not the same roads.

Not a single packet touches the public internet.

Here's how it works: you tap your card, the merchant's terminal sends a request to their bank, that bank routes it through VisaNet or Banknet to your bank, your bank approves or declines, and the whole thing completes in under 2 seconds.

These networks were built this way on purpose. The public internet wasn't designed for financial transactions at this scale. Too slow, too unreliable, too exposed.

VisaNet handles over 65,000 transactions per second at peak. 99.9999% uptime, about 30 seconds of downtime per year.

Visa makes cloud providers look like amateurs.

Visa started incorporating internet connectivity in 2021. Mastercard in 2025. Fashionably late.
