In 2019, OpenAI built an AI model and withheld it from the public.

Too dangerous, they said. It could write fake news. Generate disinformation at scale. Automate propaganda.

The media went wild. The Guardian, BBC, Wired, The Verge, all ran the story. "AI too dangerous to release" dominated tech headlines for weeks.

Researchers debated. The AI community split. The world braced for an AI that could fool anyone.

The model? **GPT-2. 1.5 billion parameters.**

For 2019, it was impressive, humans rated its text credible up to 83% of the time. But it repeated itself after a few paragraphs. Got basic facts wrong. Couldn't follow instructions. Couldn't hold a conversation.

Fast forward to today.

Open-source models with 5x the parameters run on a laptop. Llama, Qwen, DeepSeek, all free, all publicly available, all dramatically more capable than GPT-2 ever was.

The "too dangerous" model from 2019? You could run it on your phone now. It would lose to a model a fraction of its size trained with modern techniques.

The thing OpenAI was afraid of, it wasn't the model. It was the idea that AI could generate text humans couldn't tell apart from real writing.

That ship didn't just sail. It circumnavigated the globe.
