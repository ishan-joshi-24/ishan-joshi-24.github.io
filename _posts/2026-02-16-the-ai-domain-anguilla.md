A tiny Caribbean island of 15,000 people is making $93 million a year from the AI boom. They don't build AI. They don't sell AI. They just own two letters.

I was trying to buy a .ai domain.

₹7,000 per year. Two-year minimum. ₹14,000 just to own a URL.

For a domain extension? That felt steep. So I went down a rabbit hole.

Turns out, .ai has nothing to do with artificial intelligence. It's a country code. Every two-letter domain extension is. And this one belongs to Anguilla.

They were assigned .ai back in 1995, long before artificial intelligence was even a thing.

For decades, nobody cared.

Then the AI boom hit.

Suddenly every startup, every tool, every side project wanted a .ai domain. And Anguilla was sitting on a goldmine they never asked for.

The numbers are wild:

- 2018: $2.9 million in .ai domain revenue
- 2024: $39 million
- 2025: $93 million, nearly 47% of their entire national budget

A two-letter country code assigned by accident is now on track to surpass tourism as their biggest revenue source. The money is funding infrastructure, public services, and helping diversify an economy that once depended almost entirely on tourism.

All because of two letters. And a whole lot of luck.
