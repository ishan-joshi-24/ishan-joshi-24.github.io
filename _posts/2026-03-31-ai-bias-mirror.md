title: "A Mirror Can't Outgrow the Person Standing in Front of It"

description: "Amazon, Microsoft Tay, the Apple Card. Three stories about AI bias. The pattern hasn't changed."

Amazon built an AI hiring tool to find the best candidates. It taught itself to reject women.

Nobody programmed it to. It learned from 10 years of resumes. Most came from men. So the AI decided "good candidate" sounds male. It penalized the word "women's." Amazon scrapped the project in 2017.

In 2016, Microsoft launched Tay, a chatbot on Twitter designed to learn from conversations with real users. Within 16 hours it was tweeting racist, sexist, pro-Nazi content. It learned exactly what Twitter taught it.

In 2019, Apple launched a credit card powered by Goldman Sachs' algorithm. Users reported women were getting significantly lower credit limits than men with similar financials. Apple co-founder Steve Wozniak said his wife got 10x less credit than him.

None of these were programmed to be biased. They were trained on us.

You'd think we learned. We didn't.

Today's LLMs are trained on the same internet. Ask ChatGPT to write about a nurse, *she*. An engineer, *he*. Bigger models. Same bias. Faster.

Here's what people don't get about LLMs.

They don't know anything. They just know what knowing sounds like. They don't know what's fair. They know what fair sounds like. They don't know what's true. They know what true sounds like.

They take the average of everything humanity has written and done and serve it back with full confidence.

A mirror can't outgrow the person standing in front of it.
