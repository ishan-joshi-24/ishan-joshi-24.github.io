Meta didn't open-source Llama. 4chan did.

In February 2023, Meta released Llama-1 to researchers only. Application form, license, the whole approval process.

A week later, the weights showed up as a torrent on 4chan.

That single leak rewrote the AI industry.

Within weeks, Stanford fine-tuned it into Alpaca for about $600 total. Georgi Gerganov shipped `llama.cpp`, making it run on a MacBook. Then Vicuna. Then WizardLM. Then a thousand fine-tunes.

The entire local LLM ecosystem, Ollama, LM Studio, every "run AI on your laptop" tool, traces back to that torrent.

Mistral was founded a few months later by researchers from Meta and DeepMind who saw exactly what was happening. They went open-weights from day one.

Then Meta did something most corporations wouldn't. **They leaned in.**

Llama 2 dropped in July 2023. Open weights. Then Llama 3. Then 3.1, 3.2, 3.3.

The biggest open-source movement in AI started with a leak Meta didn't want.

It ended with Meta becoming its leader.
